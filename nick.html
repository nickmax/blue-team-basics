Fake messages, and how I stopped falling for them.

I get two or three phishing emails a week. Most are easy to spot, but one almost got me last semester because it looked like a real RIT notice. So I wrote down what gave it away. This is the short version of what I check now before I click anything.

Four red flags

It is always urgent

Payroll closes in ten minutes. Your account gets suspended tonight. Real offices give you days, so the rush is the trick.

A logo proves nothing

Anyone can copy a logo and a job title. If the message asks for a password or a code, it is not really IT.

Links that almost match

rit.edu and rit-edu.support are two different places. Read the address right before the first slash.

Files you did not ask for

An invoice you never ordered, or a CV from a stranger. Check with the sender on a number you already have.

What I do now

  1. Stop. I do not reply, click or call the number in the message.
  2. I look the office up myself and call that number instead.
  3. I report the message, then delete it.
